## Leadership Principles

## Situation

- **My role:** I was a student developer on a Semantic Web Mining project at ASU.
- **Where:** I worked in a six-person team for four months.
- **Project:** We were building a fake-review detection system.
- **Team structure:** We divided the work across research, data preparation, model training, evaluation, and deployment.
- **Problem:** One teammate's model-training and evaluation checkpoints started slipping.
- **Why it mattered:** His output was needed before the team could combine and evaluate the complete system.

## Task

- **My duties:** I was responsible for completing my own assigned part of the project.
- **Team responsibility:** I also needed to help protect our shared course deadline.
- **Boundary:** I did not want to take over my teammate's entire model.
- **Goal:** I wanted to help the team recover without creating another delay in my own work.

## Action

### 1. I protected my existing commitment

- **What:** I completed my assigned work before taking on anything else.
- **How:** I checked that my work was ready for the team's next integration step.
- **Why:** Helping elsewhere would not be useful if it caused my own work to slip.

### 2. I spoke with my teammate

- **What:** I offered to help after seeing that his checkpoints were slipping.
- **How:** I spoke with him directly about his remaining work and other course deadlines.
- **Why:** I wanted to understand the constraint instead of assuming he was not trying.

### 3. I divided the immediate work

- **What:** We split his remaining task into smaller parts.
- **How:** I took one part while he kept responsibility for the rest of his model.
- **Why:** This protected the immediate checkpoint without taking over his entire responsibility.

### 4. I checked the work before integration

- **What:** I verified his output before we combined it with the project.
- **How:** I reviewed the completed model-training and evaluation output.
- **Why:** Moving faster would not help if incorrect work blocked the team later.

### 5. I recognized the repeated planning problem

- **What:** I stopped treating the second delay as another isolated rescue.
- **How:** I compared the repeated delays with the way we had divided the work.
- **Why:** Last-minute help could protect one checkpoint but would not fix unrealistic workload planning.

### 6. I reviewed the team's real capacity

- **What:** I asked each teammate about their availability.
- **How:** We discussed daily capacity and major deadlines from other courses.
- **Why:** Equal task counts did not mean everyone had equal time available.

### 7. I proposed a new work distribution

- **What:** I suggested redistributing the remaining work.
- **How:** I matched tasks with the time each person could realistically contribute.
- **Why:** A realistic plan gave us a better chance of meeting the remaining checkpoints.

## Result

- **Immediate outcome:** The team agreed to the revised work distribution.
- **Delivery:** We started meeting our weekly checkpoints again.
- **Final outcome:** We completed the project by the course deadline.
- **Team impact:** We protected the deadline without blaming the teammate or taking away all of his responsibility.
- **Learning:** Equal task distribution does not always create a fair or reliable plan.
- **What I would do differently:** I would discuss availability and other major commitments when assigning work at the beginning.

## Supporting information

### Specific data

- Four-month project
- Six team members
- One delayed model-training and evaluation responsibility
- Internal checkpoints slipped, but the final course deadline did not

### Ownership boundaries

- I completed my work before helping.
- I helped twice but did not complete my teammate's entire model.
- My teammate remained responsible for his work.
- The team agreed to redistribute the remaining tasks.

### Alternatives considered

- Continue rescuing individual tasks whenever a checkpoint slipped.
- Leave the teammate to recover alone.
- Redistribute the remaining work based on actual capacity.
- I selected the third approach because it addressed the planning problem.

### Feedback

- My teammate agreed to split the work.
- The team agreed to the revised allocation.
- No manager or professor feedback has been confirmed.

### Memory chain

> Six people → checkpoint slips → finish my work → help twice → capacity discussion → redistribute work → deadline met
