# Evidence behind the drafting decisions

## Wellfound extension — October 5, 2026

[Official guidance on effective application notes](https://help.wellfound.com/article/793-how-do-i-write-an-effective-note-to-send-with-my-application)

Wellfound recommends showing role-specific qualification in the first two lines,
direct language without jargon, impact rather than repeated job history, and
proofreading. Its suggestion to quantify impact remains subject to candidate
fact authority and legibility. The guidance was updated February 2023; it is
platform advice, not evidence of a universally successful message formula.

[How to apply](https://help.wellfound.com/article/707-how-to-start-applying-to-jobs)
and [employer receipt of applications](https://help.wellfound.com/article/764-check-applications)

The application note should respond directly to the listing. Submission makes
the job profile and any uploaded resume available to the company. Do not claim
that employers must accept a message before they can view the profile.

[Wellfound Reach applicant review](https://help.wellfound.com/article/1141-how-can-i-review-applicants-in-recruitercloud-inbound)

Reach can sort applicants into fit buckets using configured criteria. This is
one product workflow, not proof that every company uses it or that a founder
reads each note first. Writing quality cannot fix an eligibility mismatch.

[Recruiter views on cover letters](https://wellfound.com/blog/should-you-bother-with-a-cover-letter-recruiters-weigh-in)

Wellfound's recruiter compilation shows conflicting views about reading cover
letters. Its recommendations favor short, specific, personal context. Do not
generalize its advice to skip optional cover letters to a required interest
field, or treat its AI-authorship judgments as reliable detection evidence.

[WRITER recruiter on proof of work](https://wellfound.com/blog/proof-beats-polish-how-to-stand-out-when-500-people-apply-from-writer-ais)

The recruiter favors accessible examples of work and evidence of applied
learning. An existing relevant artifact may strengthen an application; the
account does not justify requiring a demo for every answer, AI-agent claims
for unrelated roles, or treating applicant-volume rhetoric as actual odds.

[Discord recruiter interview](https://wellfound.com/blog/nobodys-blacklisted-a-discord-recruiting-lead-on-outreach-ai-and-what)
and [engineering recruiter interview](https://wellfound.com/blog/red-flags-and-green-flags-what-engineers-actually-need-to-know-about-getting)

These individual accounts support adding context beyond the resume, using the
candidate's own voice, and balancing learning with initiative and communication.
They do not establish the exact sequence followed by each employer.

Implication for the company-interest field: answer the motivation question,
establish relevant ability early, and show how the role's actual working model
fits the candidate. Technical contribution supports the explanation; a stack
list, flattery, or generic desire to learn does not explain the choice.

Searches of Reddit and LinkedIn yielded limited verifiable founder-side evidence
specific to this field. Candidate anecdotes and profile-view counters cannot
establish screening order, rejection cause, or a reliable reply rate.

Research reviewed October 1, 2026. This is a qualitative basis for judgment,
not a validated reply-rate model or a universal account of founder preferences.
Use research for message decisions, never for candidate or company facts.

## Stronger source: YC hiring guidance

[Ryan Choi, Advice for job seekers on resumes and standing out, May 19, 2020](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/advice-for-job-seekers-on-resumes-and-standing-out)

YC recommends contextualizing previous work: what was done, how, and impact,
including cross-functional work. Its outreach guidance recommends explaining
where candidates would like to contribute and why. It describes hiring examples
where initiative and relevant artifacts helped candidates stand out.

Implication: connect technical evidence to understandable work and contribution.
Do not turn its recommendation of multiple contribution ideas into a compulsory
list for short notes. Its resume guidance is useful context, not a rule that
every outreach message must reproduce a resume bullet.

## Reddit: useful anecdotes with unverified identities

[Founders: what cold messages from engineers actually get your attention?](https://www.reddit.com/r/startups/comments/1qy00xs/founders_what_cold_messages_from_engineers/)

One self-described founder emphasizes current company relevance and warns
against certainty without context. Comments favor relevant artifacts with an
explanation over generic profiles and long biographies. A self-described
recruiter advocates short messages with concrete evidence.

[Startup founders: what actually makes you respond to cold outreach?](https://www.reddit.com/r/startups/comments/1wan40g/startup_founders_what_actually_makes_you_respond/)

The poster reports poor results despite offering product improvements. Responses
vary: some favor relevant demonstrations, some direct introductions, and some
report ignoring cold outreach because of volume. This is evidence of differing
preferences, not proof that any suggested tactic causes replies.

Implication: no mandatory product critique, no assumed company pain, and no
guarantees. A useful link can help when it already exists and directly relates
to the opening; building unpaid custom demos is not a default requirement.

## LinkedIn: directness can be sufficient

[Erik Stout, If you are interested in a role at a startup, reach out to the founder and ask for a conversation](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/erik-stout_if-you-are-interested-in-a-role-at-a-startup-activity-7399156341774405632-0zhC)

The author gives an outreach example naming an opening, connecting relevant
experience, and asking for a conversation, with a reported response. This
supports allowing a direct hiring introduction. It does not establish a typical
reply rate or justify requiring a meeting request in a connection note.

Other searched LinkedIn advice promoted personalization, portfolios, and custom
work, sometimes with unsupported guarantees. Do not encode guarantees or
expensive custom-work requirements.

## Glassdoor search limitation

[Discussion asking about LinkedIn networking messages](https://www.glassdoor.sg/Community/career-advice-for-students/do-you-have-a-tried-and-true-linkedin-message-that-gets-you-actual-responses-when-youre-trying-to-network-or-outreach-if)

Targeted searches yielded little directly relevant founder-side engineering
selection evidence. Sales prospecting and freelance anecdotes were excluded
from the drafting rules because they do not establish startup hiring behavior.

## Practical conclusions and user-specific choices

Research supports relevance, contextual evidence, concise communication, and
accessible proof of work. It does not determine a fixed technical/product ratio,
opening line, story structure, question requirement, or reliable reply target.

The user's choices supply channel limits, casual voice, no em dashes, no project
stories in short connection notes, naming the role in LinkedIn messages, and
questions only when useful. These are preferences, not experimentally proven
founder preferences. Technical details are retained when they explain a relevant
contribution. Review clarity before polish or apparent impressiveness.
